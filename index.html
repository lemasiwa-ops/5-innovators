01 · The opportunity

The market already has cards. The opportunity is to own capability.

Revolut has normalised child debit cards. Money Smarts has established financial education in schools. The strategic opening is the space between them: everyday practice at home.

Financial literacy
54%

Average financial-literacy score reported in Bank of Ireland / Red C research published in February 2026.

Saving was identified as the weakest knowledge area.

Education reach
600k+

Students participated in Bank of Ireland Money Smarts between September 2019 and May 2025.

Financial education already has strong institutional credibility.

Family lifecycle
6–17

A twelve-year customer-development window in which the bank can grow with the child and deepen the family relationship.

The opportunity extends beyond a single card product.

02 · Competitive reality

Do not out-Revolut Revolut.

Competing on card functionality alone leads directly into an established fintech battleground. The differentiation must be broader and more defensible.

Revolut Kids & Teens

Digital-first

Owns: frictionless supervised spending.

  • Strong consumer familiarity
  • Parent visibility and controls
  • Already serves ages 6–17

Strategic gap: deeper learning, family routines and a broader trusted-bank relationship.

GoHenry

Learning-led

Owns: earn, spend, save and learn.

  • Chore-linked money
  • Structured financial education
  • Strong parent proposition

Strategic gap: local Irish banking relationship and ecosystem scale.

Greenlight

Family platform

Owns: a broad family-money management platform.

  • Family-level pricing
  • Multiple children
  • Strong household management model

Strategic gap: not an Irish proposition and not embedded in the domestic banking or education environment.

03 · Recommended positioning

Own the progression, not the plastic.

A six-year-old learning to save and a sixteen-year-old preparing for financial independence require fundamentally different experiences.

Positioning platform
“The money-learning account that grows up with your child.”

School can teach the concepts. Home is where children practise them. The bank becomes the trusted bridge between the two.

04 · Product proposition

One family platform. Increasing independence over time.

The value is not the individual feature. It is how the features work together as a child's capability develops.

01

Learn through real money

Turn allowances, chores, savings and spending into short, practical money-learning moments.

02

Give parents confidence

Set limits, manage cards, automate allowances and retain visibility while children learn to make their own decisions.

03

Grow with the child

Progress from simple earning and saving at six to stronger budgeting, decision-making and independence in the teenage years.

05 · A typical money moment

The lesson happens inside everyday life.

Tuesday

“Take out the bins — €2.”

A parent creates a simple chore. The child sees a clear link between contribution and earning.

Friday

Allowance arrives.

The child's balance updates automatically and their savings goal becomes visible.

Saturday

A real choice appears.

Spend now, save some, or move more towards something they genuinely want.

Learning moment

The app connects action to understanding.

Short prompts explain saving, budgeting and delayed gratification in the context of the decision just made.

08 · Go-to-market

Launch the idea of financial confidence — not another banking feature.

The strongest launch combines bank trust, parental relevance and a highly relatable family-money moment.

Lead with a parent truth

“Kids don't learn money by watching a balance. They learn it by making choices.”

Show the experience

Make the campaign tangible: Tuesday's chore, Friday's allowance, Saturday's decision.

Own the progression

Demonstrate how the experience changes from first savings goal to teenage financial independence.

Board decision

Build a family relationship that can last beyond childhood.

This is more than a youth-acquisition product. Done well, it becomes the first stage of a lifetime banking relationship — built with the parent, practised by the child and progressively transferred to the young adult.

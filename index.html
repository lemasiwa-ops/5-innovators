What this looks like at home

Tuesday: the bins go out.
Friday: €2 comes in.

1 · Earn You add a chore: “Take out the bins — €2.”
2 · Receive Once it's done, the money arrives in their account.
3 · Decide They choose whether to spend it or move some towards a savings goal.
4 · Learn A short prompt helps them understand the choice they just made.

The lesson isn't separate from real life. The lesson is the decision.

Questions you may have

Before you hand them their first card…

Is six too young for this?

For younger children, the experience is deliberately simple and parent-led. The focus is on earning, saving and understanding choices — not unrestricted spending.

Will I still be able to control what they spend?

Yes. Parent controls and visibility remain central to the experience. You decide when they are ready for more independence.

Why not just give them cash?

Cash can teach valuable lessons too. The app adds visibility, savings goals, regular allowances and opportunities to talk about decisions together.

Will this mean even more screen time?

The experience is designed around short interactions connected to real life: complete a chore, check a goal, make a purchase, review what's left.

What happens as they get older?

Their experience evolves. You can gradually move from close supervision towards greater independence as their judgement and confidence develop.
